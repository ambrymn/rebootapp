## Design Context

### Users
SleepBand is for people roughly age 15 and up who want sleep tracking to feel approachable instead of clinical. Users are checking sleep quality, evening phone habits, streaks, rewards, and eventually wearable connection state from a portrait-first mobile app. The app should help them understand last night's rest, feel nudged toward better wind-down habits, and stay engaged through quests without making sleep feel like a chore.

### Brand Personality
Sleepy, gamified, cozy. The product should feel like a soft nighttime game layered over practical wellness tracking: rewarding, friendly, and a little magical, but still clear enough for everyday use. It should create calm and motivation at the same time.

### Aesthetic Direction
Start dark-only. The visual direction should preserve the current sleep-at-night context while moving away from generic neon dashboard tropes. Favor cozy depth, tactile game UI details, soft contrast, and distinctive reward systems over glassy cards, decorative blobs, or template-like gradients. The app should not look like generic AI-generated wellness software.

### Design Principles
- Make sleep feel rewarding, not medical: use quests, progress, streaks, and rewards to create motivation without pressure.
- Keep the mood cozy at night: dark surfaces, softened contrast, and warm interaction details should support late-evening use.
- Design for teens and adults together: playful enough to engage younger users, polished enough not to feel childish.
- Avoid AI-slop patterns: no generic glowing dark dashboard look, no decorative card grids, no gratuitous gradients, and no filler visual effects.
- Keep the product usable first: every game mechanic should clarify sleep behavior or encourage healthier routines.
